# The Tapestry

The world behind the gold pool in the wood: a long strip of embroidered linen,
read from left to right, that teaches medieval history. The player is the
scribe who finishes it.

Four acts are made so far, thirty-eight panels and 131 questions. The cloth
opens on the first:

- **I · Rome's Shadow** (nine panels): the empire at its height; whether Rome
  fell or changed; what it left behind; the Church stepping in; the monks who
  kept the books; the Church in everyday life; Charlemagne crowned; the
  eastern half that carried on; and Justinian, told twice by the same writer.

- **II · The Viking Thread** (eight panels): who the Northmen were, the raid
  on Lindisfarne, the road east to Baghdad's silver, settlement from the
  Danelaw to Newfoundland, Cnut and Emma, Rollo's bargain on the Seine, how
  the Northmen of Normandy became French, and William's childhood.
- **III · The Year 1066** (twelve panels): from King Edward dying without an
  heir to William crowned on Christmas Day.
- **IV · What the Conquest Changed** (nine panels): castles; the Harrying of
  the North; who owned the land; Domesday Book; French above and English
  below; the new stone churches; the king's forests; the village behind the
  plough; and the reckoning at William's death.

The first panel of each act is open from the start, so any can be begun
first. Within an act the panels unlock in order.

## One cloth, changing borders

The scribe, the linen and the stitches are the same throughout. But each act
borrows its borders and its motifs from the people it is about, and says so
at its head:

- **Rome's Shadow** has the key-pattern border of Roman floors, little columns
  between eagles, dolphins, peacocks and lambs, temples and aqueducts, and
  the gold haloes and domes of Constantinople.
- **1066** follows the Bayeux Tapestry itself: slanting bars in the borders,
  and its birds, lions, dragons, hounds and fish.
- **What the Conquest Changed** has the zigzag the Normans cut round their
  doors, round-headed arches between the beasts, castles of earth and timber
  and great churches; and its border beasts are the ones Domesday counted and
  the king kept for himself: ox, pig, stag and hare.
- **The Viking Thread** has plaited borders, knots between ravens, wolves and
  ribbon-beasts, ships with chequered sails as on the Gotland picture-stones,
  and rune-stones. There is no tapestry of these events, so every Latin
  caption in that act is the scribe's own.

## How it works

- Each panel starts as an under-drawing on bare linen.
- At the desk below the cloth she reads a short account of what happened, with
  where the account comes from.
- Then come two to four questions. Each right answer is **stitched while she
  watches**: shape by shape, in the order the needle would take them, each
  filled from the top down. When a stretch is done, the figures in it stir.
- A wrong answer is stitched as **a small picture of the mistake itself** (say
  William brought elephants, and elephants are what you get), which shivers
  and is pulled off. The beasts of the border lean in: the Bird Who Is Always
  Wrong agrees with her, and then the lion or the wyrm gives a real hint.
- When a panel is finished the desk says why it mattered, and adds one thing
  that is **true, and odd**, with who said so.

## Playing with it

None of this is examined. It is kept apart from the history on purpose: jokes
are shown as speech or as pictures, never as fact.

- **Poke anyone** in a stitched picture. They hop (horses rear) and say
  something.
- **The beasts.** Eight to collect, one every few panels. The lion button on
  the desk shows them. Tap a beast in the border under a finished panel to
  change it for one of yours.
- **Runes.** One panel carries a real runic inscription. The desk has the
  sixteen runes with their sounds, and a word of it to decode letter by
  letter.

survives. Rest the pointer on a caption, or tap it, to see the language, a
translation, and the source; or, if the caption was written for this cloth,

How well each is checked. In Act I: Virgil, the Rule of Benedict, the Frankish
annals and Einhard were read in the Latin texts themselves; Jerome's line was
confirmed in translation, and its Latin wording and the Greek of Procopius
are given as commonly cited. In Act II: the Old English lines and the rune-stone's reading
were confirmed against search results quoting editions. The charter's wording
is as scholars cite it. The Arabic line matched the text of the standard
edition in a search, but the edition itself could not be opened from here.
A second rune-stone that was wanted could not be confirmed and was left out.
In Act IV: the four Chronicle lines were read in Plummer and Earle's edition
of the Old English, and Robert of Gloucester's in Wright's edition (both
scanned at archive.org); Orderic's figure was read in Forester's translation.
Malmesbury's Latin and the London writ are as quoted in recent scholarly
articles found by search. `WASTA EST` and `TERRA REGIS` are Domesday's own
standing phrases, given from general knowledge.

## The look

- The ground is a photograph of real linen (see `textures/CREDITS.md`).
- Every laid thread is drawn as its own strand: a shade lighter or darker than
  its neighbours, uneven along its length, lit on top and shadowed below, and
  held down by short stitches across. Outlines are a rope of stem stitch. The
  wool throws a soft shadow on the cloth.
- There are no colour gradients, because the real cloth has none.
- Each scene is scattered with birds, plants and stones.

**Whose account?** Eleven panels are told twice, from sources that disagree:

| Panel | One telling | The other |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Did Rome fall? | it fell (Jerome; the archaeology) | it changed (the Gothic kingdoms; modern historians) |
| Charlemagne crowned | his court's annals: a proud public moment | his friend Einhard: a surprise he disliked |
| Justinian | Procopius in public: lawgiver and builder | Procopius in secret: a demon with no head |
| The raid on Lindisfarne | the monks (the Chronicle; Alcuin) | what the Northmen left: no words, only things |
| Rollo's bargain | Dudo's story, written for Rollo's descendants | a dry charter of 918 |
| Harold's oath | the Normans (William of Poitiers) | the English (the Chronicle's silence; Eadmer) |
| Stamford Bridge | the English (Anglo-Saxon Chronicle) | the Northmen (Snorri's saga, c. 1230) |
| Harold's death | the arrow (the tapestry; Amatus; Baudri) | the sword (the Carmen, c. 1067) |
| The Harrying of the North | Orderic Vitalis: a hundred thousand dead | Domesday: one word, "waste", and what it can and cannot prove |
| Domesday Book | the English chronicler: shameful prying | the king's treasurer, a century on: justice |
| Behind the plough | what was lost: freedom, and the murdrum fine | what stayed (shires, laws, coin) and what ended (slavery) |

Both tellings must be stitched, side by side in the same panel, and then one
more question asks her to weigh them: what they agree on, what they differ
on, and why.

Two panels (the three claimants, and Stamford Bridge) are not in the real
tapestry at all, and say so. That absence is itself one of the questions in
the last panel, which is about the cloth as an object: who paid for it, who
stitched it, and what it leaves out.

## Moving

- ◀ ▶, the arrow keys, a swipe or the scroll wheel move along the cloth.
- The green button goes home. **new** unpicks everything. ♪ is the sound.

## Sources, and what is certain

- **The Latin captions** are the tapestry's own, taken from F. F. L. Birrell's
  *Guide to the Bayeux Tapestry* (Victoria and Albert Museum, 1921), except
  three written for panels the tapestry does not have: `TRES REGNUM PETUNT`,
  `HIC HAROLD REX NORTHMANNOS VICIT`, and the banners over the paired
  tellings (`NORMANNI DICUNT`, `ANGLI DICUNT`, `NORTHMANNI DICUNT`, `SAGITTA`,
  `GLADIUS`). The game says which are which. `ET FUGA VERTERUNT ANGLI`, the
  last surviving caption, is not in Birrell's list and is given from general
  knowledge.
- **Quotations from the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle** are from James Ingram's
  translation (1823), checked against the Project Gutenberg text (the
  entries for 793, 1017, 1065 and 1066).
- **In the Viking act**, Alcuin, Ibn Fadlan, Dudo of Saint-Quentin and the
  charter of 918 are described or given in reported speech. The Newfoundland
  date of 1021 is from the tree-ring study published in *Nature* in 2021.
- **Everything else** is a summary of what is generally taught. The Norman
  writers (William of Poitiers, William of Jumièges), Eadmer, John of
  Worcester, the Carmen, Amatus, Baudri and Snorri are described, not quoted.
  The saga's "seven feet of English ground" is given in reported speech.
- Where historians disagree the text says so: whether Edward died on the 4th
  or 5th of January; who crowned Harold; whether the first Norman retreat at
  Hastings was a trick or a panic; the date of the Carmen; how Harold died;
  who commissioned the tapestry and where it was made.

## What is remembered

In this browser only, and saved after every right answer and every move along
the cloth: which parts of which panels are stitched, how many questions were
answered right at the first attempt, which beasts she has put in which
borders, whether the runes are decoded, and which panel she was on.

The chips at the top of the desk jump to any act; the first panel of each is
always open. To start again, press the lion button: at the foot of that page
are **Unpick only this act** and **Unpick the whole cloth**.

## Files

- `extras.ts`: everything playful: the odd facts, what each figure says when
  poked, the rules for silly wrong answers, and the beasts.
- `acts.ts`: the acts, and the history of 1066. Every caption, account,
  source note, question and hint. The first answer listed for each question
  is the right one.
- `rome.ts`, `vikings.ts`, `conquest.ts`: the same for Acts I, II and IV.
- `stitch.ts`: drawing as embroidery, thread by thread, and a record of each
  stitch so that the stitching can be played back.
- `scenes.ts`: the figures, horses, ships and buildings, the twelve scenes,
  and the creatures of the border.
- `main.ts`: the cloth, the desk, stitching, sound, and what is remembered.

For development: `?fresh` ignores what is remembered, `?all` shows everything
stitched, `?at=6` opens on that panel.
